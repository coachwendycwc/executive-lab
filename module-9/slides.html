The Framework

Three Structures: STAR · CAR · CARL

STAR

Situation · Task · Action · Result

The full frame. Best for interviews and reviews where context matters.

CAR

Challenge · Action · Result

The tight frame. Best when time is short and the result should land fast.

CARL

Challenge · Action · Result · Learning

Adds reflection. Best for growth conversations and leading teams.

Same win, three frames

STAR A vendor migration had stalled for two quarters (Situation). I was asked to ship it without slipping the date (Task). I pulled the three teams into one room and re-sequenced the work into phases (Action). We shipped two weeks early (Result).

CAR A migration everyone had given up on was two quarters behind (Challenge). I re-sequenced it into phases and got the teams in one room (Action). We shipped two weeks early (Result).

CARL Same as CAR, plus the lesson: a stalled project is usually a coordination problem, not a technical one (Learning).

Strategy

Owning Your Narrative as a Woman of Color

The Realities

  • Many of us are taught to deflect credit, to say “we” when it was you.
  • Centering others can quietly erase your own role in the win.
  • Minimizing (“it was nothing”) reads as fact, not modesty.

The Moves

  • Claim your actions in the first person: “I led,” “I decided,” “I built.”
  • Credit the team and keep your part visible; both can be true.
  • State the result plainly. Owning it authentically is not bragging.
ELL
Module 9 — September
Technique

How You Tell It Is Half of It

Ownership language

“I led,” not “we kind of.” Cut the hedges that shrink your role.

Pacing & pause

Slow down at the result. Let the important beat land before you move on.

Presence

Steady voice, eye contact, no rushing to fill silence. Conviction is heard.

A strong story told apologetically loses. The delivery is what makes the win believable.
