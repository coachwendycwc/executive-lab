Sponsor vs. Mentor Guide

Mentors talk with you. Sponsors talk about you when you're not in the room. Career growth needs both.

How to use this guide

Start with the distinction, then audit who is actually on your side today. Women of color are often over-mentored and under-sponsored: rich in advice, short on advocacy. This guide helps you name the gap and plan the ask. Answers auto-save as you type.

1
The Distinction
Two different relationships, two different kinds of help. You need a mix of the two.
Mentor
Talks WITH you
What they do
Advise, coach, share experience, help you think.
Where the value is
Your growth, skills, and judgment.
Ask them for
Feedback, perspective, a sounding board.
You earn it by
Being coachable and following through.
Sponsor
Talks ABOUT you
What they do
Advocate for you and spend their capital on your advancement.
Where the value is
Rooms and decisions you're not in.
Ask them for
A specific opportunity, a name put forward, air cover.
You earn it by
Delivering visible results they can vouch for.
2
Who's Actually On Your Side?
List the people invested in your career. Be honest about the role each truly plays, and name one thing each has done for you when you weren't there. If you can't name one, they're likely a mentor, not a sponsor.
Note: Not all mentors or sponsors are at your current organization. Former managers, industry peers, and people at other companies count too, so include them here.

3
The Advice-vs-Advocacy Gap
Over-mentored and under-sponsored is a pattern, not a personal failing. It's how advocacy tends to get distributed. Naming the gap is how you close it.
4
Turn a Mentor Into a Sponsor
Sponsorship is earned and asked for. Pick one person with real power, make your ask specific, and give them the exact language to use on your behalf.
5
My Commitment
One concrete move beats a perfect plan. Make it small enough to actually do this month.
